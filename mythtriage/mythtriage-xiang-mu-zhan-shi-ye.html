Medical MythTriage

Can a fast decision model be trusted to pre-screen medical claims?

A fast model says how sure it is. Code decides whether that sureness is enough. On fluent claims, it isn’t — and that gap is the finding.

Motivation

According to researchers at Stanford University and Anthropic, LLMs today still go wrong — and whether a model admits it is unstable across identical training runs (Feng et al., 2026). Agents make hundreds of trust-or-verify decisions per run while verification is scarce, so this project measures when a fast model’s verdict may be trusted — and puts the decision in code.

    TL;DRRead this and you have the paper

    A fast model — the “glancer” — judges each medical claim once and states a confidence. Code, not the model, holds a gate at 0.90: high-confidence claims are settled fast, everything else goes to a slower verifier that searches the literature, with every citation checked in code, or the system abstains.

    On 619 claims the same gate behaves oppositely on two claim styles: it safely settles 16.5% of expert-written claims at perfect precision, but on fluent, natural-sounding claims its safe coverage at 95% precision is 0%.

    Latency & cost: the gate only pays where it settles a lot. On HealthFC it cuts cost 12% but saves no time — ≈4.3s vs 4.0s per claim for verifying everything, because every claim first pays the glancer’s ~0.9s and only 15.9% skip the verifier. On MEDFACT it cuts cost 60% and latency 42% (≈2.3s per claim) — but that speed and saving are bought with settled precision of just 0.712. The gate’s savings and its risk are the same number read two ways.

    The model’s confidence tracks how fluently a claim is written, not whether it is true.

    RELATED EVIDENCE

    When will an LLM admit it is wrong? Unpredictably.

    Feng et al. study failure disclosure — whether a model admits an attempted solution failed. Across 20 repeated outcome-only GRPO training runs with near-identical task accuracy, failure disclosure varied 3.9× more than solve rate (cross-run SD). Ten continuations from a single mid-training state, differing only in rollout randomness, ended with disclosure rates from 0.11 to 0.88.

    Two failure shapes, one lesson. MythTriage’s glancer is biased — its confidence tracks fluency. Feng et al.’s models drift — admission varies run to run. Either way, a model’s own say-so cannot be the safety mechanism — so the gate lives in code.

    Steven Y. Feng, Noah D. Goodman & Michael C. Frank (Stanford University) and Evan Hubinger, Paul C. Bogdan & Andrew Lampinen (Anthropic) — Feng’s work was done as part of the Anthropic Fellows program. “When Do Models Admit They Are Wrong? Failure Disclosure Is Unstable Under Reinforcement Learning.” arXiv:2609.33220 (Sep 2026). Read the paper ↗

    01

    Why this exists

    Three reasons — one personal, one observed in class, one that belongs to every agent system.

    01 — Personal

    Built for claims like hers

    “My grandmother had a remedy for everything — some of it was love, some of it was wrong, and a little of it was risky.”

    The checker was built for claims like hers: short, warm, certain — the kind that travel through families, not journals.

    02 — The discovery

    Confident error is the dangerous form

    At the start of this class, working on agent projects, I found that agents still hallucinate — and the dangerous form is confident error: they hallucinate confidently, and confidence is the very signal we route by.

    03 — The general problem

    Verification is scarce

    Every agent makes this decision hundreds of times per run — trust the fast answer, or stop and verify? Verification is the scarce resource, and the field’s default routing signal is the model’s own confidence. That signal had never been measured on medical claims against a failure line fixed in advance.

    02

    How it works

    One pass to glance, one line in code to decide, one slower pass to verify — or no answer at all.

    ◐

    1  Glance

    The glancer judges each claim once and states a verdict with a confidence score.

    Fast model · 1 call
    ⬢

    2  Gate — 0.90

    Held in code, not in the model. At or above 0.90, settle fast. Below it, do not trust the glance.

    In code · Not learned
    ◎

    3  Verify

    A slower verifier searches the literature in a bounded pass. Every citation is validated in code.

    Literature search
    ○

    4  Or abstain

    If the evidence does not check out, the system abstains rather than guessing.

    No answer > wrong answer
    03

    The case that makes it concrete

    Key case

    “Drink about two liters of water a day.”

    Glancer said: TRUE · 0.90 In the data: MYTH

    The glancer certified it true exactly at the gate. It sounds like advice you have heard a hundred times — fluent, familiar, harmless-sounding. That fluency is what the confidence was measuring.

    CITATION · MEDFACT EVIDENCE
    大多数专家都认为，我们对液体的需求取决于年龄、体型、性别、环境和运动水平。罗森伯格说：“8乘8法则的一个错误之处在于，它把人类作为有机体对所处环境的反应完全简单化了。我们应该用能量需求的方式来计算应摄入的液体量，包括我们所处的环境温度和体能活动的水平。” 大多数专家认为，我们其实不需要担心每天喝多少水。当我们口渴时，身体会发出信号，就像肚子饿了或身体累了一样。喝过量水对健康的唯一好处可就是跑厕所消耗的额外热量。
