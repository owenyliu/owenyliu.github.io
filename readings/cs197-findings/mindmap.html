《表格表示学习中的数值预处理评估》——Max Lee、Owen Liu、Rita Xiang。一个 Stanford 研究项目，为美国和中国的高中生、大学生科研新手而讲。

How to read this map这张图怎么读

Think of the AI (a VAE) as a student who may copy a hospital chart onto a tiny sticky note — only 8 numbers allowed — and later has to redraw the whole chart from that note. Our project changed just one thing: how the numbers were handed to the student. Raw? Rescaled so every column uses the same ruler? Chopped into bins? Then we graded the notes three ways: can they predict who is in danger, can they rebuild the chart, and do patients who were close in the chart stay close in the note?

把这个 AI（一个叫 VAE 的模型）想象成一个学生：他只能把一张医院病历抄到一张小小的便利贴上——只许写 8 个数字——之后还要凭这张便利贴把整张病历画回来。我们的项目只改一件事：数字是以什么形式交到他手里的。原样？先换成同一把尺子？切成一段一段的箱子？然后我们用三种方式给笔记打分：能不能预测谁有危险、能不能还原病历、原来在病历里相近的病人，在笔记里是不是还相近。

The headline is humbling: the plainest prep — standard scaling, the first recipe in every textbook — won. And the deepest lesson is the disagreement: a note can be terrible at remembering the chart and still be the best at spotting the rare patient who will die. Before you ask “is this AI good?”, ask “good at which score?”

结论有点反直觉：最朴素的预处理——标准化，课本里的第一招——赢了。而更深的一课是那场“分数打架”：一张笔记可以完全记不住病历，却最擅长找出那位罕见的、会去世的病人。所以在问“这个 AI 好不好”之前，先问一句：“在哪个分数上好？”
